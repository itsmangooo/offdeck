# Writing for OFFDECK

OFFDECK is an 18+ game for friends who can laugh at a bad decision. The voice is candid, sharp, sometimes filthy, and dry enough to let people discover the joke. Give each card an embarrassing truth, a recognizable motive or an unexpected consequence that changes the meaning of the setup. Read it in a normal speaking voice.

## Six categories

| Database category | Subject | Where to find the joke |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hookups` | Sex & Hookups | Awkward intimacy, misplaced confidence, bad timing and what adults pretend went well. |
| `dating` | Dating & Relationships | Exes, betrayal, dating apps, rebound logic and the effort it takes to maintain a terrible lie. |
| `expectations` | Men vs. Women | Attraction, contradictory expectations and habits couples learn about each other. Make the behavior or the situation the target. |
| `dark` | Dark Humor | Mortality, bad luck, greed and the gap between a catastrophe and the petty thing somebody cares about. |
| `social` | Social Disasters | Family secrets, bad manners, public humiliation and the moment a room finds out something private. |
| `unhinged` | Unhinged | Take an absurd premise seriously enough that its consequences make sense. |

Profanity and sexual references belong in the deck when they improve the line. Use consenting adults in sexual situations. Reject targeted hate, sexual violence, exploitation of minors and dehumanization.

## Prompts

- Set up one situation. Stop as soon as the players know what kind of embarrassment, temptation or disaster is at stake.
- Vary direct questions with messages, overheard dialogue, announcements, hypotheticals, statements and confessions. A fill-in uses one `____` and works when the card and response are read in sequence.
- Use words people would say together. Read the prompt aloud with a person or object, an action and an admission. Look for at least two genuinely playable openings across several responses. Retire prompts that only work with a particular prop or verb tense.
- Leave room for the response to reveal a secret or set up a second meaning. Caps and vulgarity can serve a voice. Extra scene-setting usually delays the payoff.

## Responses

- Aim for two to twelve words. Keep the extra words when removing them would take out the turn. Read the ending aloud and cut the explanation after the laugh.
- Give a hand different tools. Include people, objects, actions, short dialogue, admissions and consequences. Use fragments when a fragment is how someone would say it.
- Make the first image clear. “Dressed for my husband's acquittal” suggests an entire story in five words. An irrelevant appliance with an adjective makes players invent the story and the joke.
- Treat recurring devices as a small part of the deck. Workplace systems, spreadsheets, therapy, HR, PowerPoint and appliance jokes need a distinct observation to earn a slot.
- Test a line against prompts from its own category and at least two others. A flexible line can answer literally or reveal something the reader didn't expect. A niche line needs a payoff strong enough to justify the slot.

## Review calibration

| Prompt from the audit | Responses tried | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “What would you sneak into your own obituary if nobody could stop you?” | “A coupon for one free alibi.” / “Three divorces and a podcast.” / a new draft “Cause of death withheld until sweeps week.” | Keep the prompt. The entries can be a dubious accomplishment, a confession or a posthumous publicity grab. The first is playable and stranger than the obvious answer. |
| “What's living under the sink that my landlord insists is a tenant?” | “Breathing so heavily I got a referral to a pulmonologist.” / “A safe word neither of us could spell.” / “A gumball machine that takes teeth.” | Retire the prompt. Even the closest answer needs players to accept a second unexplained premise. The breath line reads like a story being explained and is also retired. |
| “Who made the mistake of asking why I'm still single?” | “My boyfriend's better-dressed identical twin.” / “An executioner on restrictive light duties.” / “An air fryer and the exact same bullshit” as a test paraphrase. | Keep the prompt and the first two responses. Both point to people and give the table a problem to fill in. The appliance is a mismatch and the deck already has several variants of that device. |
| “What did Grandma ask me to explain slowly and at full volume?” | “Office hours at the glory hole.” / “My parents' life insurance agent liking my thirst trap.” / “Merging households and discovering we had six sandwich presses.” | Keep the prompt. The first two invite a confession or a terrible explanation in front of family. The third has no turn with or without this prompt. |

Use the audit before editing a batch. Keep strong lines where they still serve the collection. Keep the ID for an edit to the same card. Retire an ID with its premise and use a fresh ID for a new card.
