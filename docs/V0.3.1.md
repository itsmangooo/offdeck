## v0.3.0 content audit

The [card-by-card audit](content-audit-0.3.0.csv) covers the 1,000 cards from release commit `914d872`. A keep has a clear joke or setup that holds up aloud. A rewrite has a usable premise buried under weak wording or an extra clause. A remove retires a repetitive, narrow or low-payoff idea.

| Card type | Keep | Rewrite | Remove |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Scenario | 45 | 155 | 50 |
| Response | 197 | 330 | 223 |

- **Nearly every setup sounds the same.** All 250 scenarios are questions and 248 start with “What”. Introduce overheard lines, fill-ins, messages and confessions. Players should hear the tension before they start looking for an answer.
- **Specific details can make a prompt a guessing game.** `scenario-211` needs a thing that lives under a sink and could plausibly be a tenant. A landlord, a sink and a tenant joke are already three decisions made for the table. The category needs broader excuses for chaos.
- **Responses often explain their own punchline.** 107 exceed 12 words, including 38 in social and 29 in expectations. `response-121` explains a medical referral where an embarrassing sound or physical reaction would do. `response-230` gives an exact appliance count without a meaningful turn. Shortening it would preserve the same weak idea, so both cards are marked remove.
- **Verbal and subject repetition hide behind unique text.** 477 responses begin with “A”, “An”, “The”, or “My”. That opening can suit a flexible noun phrase, but entire runs of overqualified props sound alike. Corporate phrasing, paperwork, funerals with extra charges, dentists and parking form repeated joke families. Limit each device and vary who is speaking, what they did, and what they were trying to conceal.
- **Keep sharp, legible lines.** A safe word neither of us could spell (`response-001`) and “I've been banned from stronger establishments” (`response-631`) create an image without explaining it. The obituary prompt (`scenario-127`) has recognizable stakes and accepts objects, admissions and dialogue.

## Verification record

- **1. Audit** reviewed all 250 scenarios and 750 responses by category, scanned length, openings and recurring subjects, and checked that the audit contains exactly one decision for each v0.3.0 card.

- **2. Humor identity** wrote and applied the six-category style guide to a calibration set of actual v0.3.0 combinations. The examples show how the review distinguishes a playable surprise from a narrow or flat pairing.
- **3. Scenarios** kept 45 strong originals, revised 155 prompts, and wrote 50 replacements with new IDs. The 250 scenarios include 60 questions, 106 fill-ins and 84 open setups. Reviewed the six batches for spoken phrasing, setup length and literal or unexpected answers. All 26 Node tests passed. A three-client production match finished and showed the new open setup in 3D inspection.
- **4. Responses** kept 197 originals, rewrote 330 responses and added 223 new ones. Reviewed all six batches for audible cadence, varied punchline forms and repeated props. 733 of 750 responses are two to twelve words. All 27 Node tests and a full three-client physical match passed. The 3D readability test confirmed hand, voting and results at three desktop sizes. Refined the test to measure the printable area instead of treating a one-pixel font overhang as clipping.
- **5. Database** migrated the two source JSON collections to typed records with stable IDs, categories, status and optional tags. The loader rejected malformed, invalid and duplicate records. Unit tests verified draft and disabled records are excluded from runtime and metadata stays out of card snapshots. All 28 Node tests, the production build and both the default-deck and complete three-client tests passed.
- **6. Compatibility** applied optional response-form notes to representative cards and inspected 60 seeded, cross-category pairings. Three scenarios were refined based on the review. The 29 Node tests passed and the approved runtime decks were byte-equivalent with and without editorial tags and compatibility metadata. The match engine continues to shuffle cards normally.
- **7. Editor** exercised the localhost editor in Chromium against a temporary copy of the actual source files. Search, all filters, shared-renderer preview, duplicate warnings, create, edit, disable, reload, canceled delete and confirmed delete passed. The API tests checked cross-origin requests, alternate hosts, stale writes, atomic JSON persistence and route isolation from production. All 31 Node tests, the production build and both focused browser tests passed. Inspected the editor screenshot and corrected the reload button contrast.
- **8. Validation** checked all 1,000 approved records for IDs, status, category, format, text and exact and likely duplicates. Reviewed 60 more pairings sampled from the full pool and edited four responses and two prompts. All 32 Node tests passed. Chromium drew all 1,000 card faces and 750 scored response faces, checking the full text, type size and ink bounds. The live three-viewport readability test passed.
- **9. Integration** passed 33 Node tests and 22 production Chromium tests. Eight connected WebSocket clients completed the full 20-scenario default game, kept five unique responses each, scored identically and finished by deck exhaustion. Three browsers completed the physical-card game and two fresh rematches. The suite also verified replenishment, recycling, anonymity, self-vote prevention, reconnects and host migration. Inspected hand spacing, inspection, voting and scored cards at 1280 x 720, 1440 x 900 and 1920 x 1080.
